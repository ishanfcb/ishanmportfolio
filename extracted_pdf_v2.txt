Project title: Digital Fatigue
Tags: Research, UX, UI
1. Context
Type
Group Project (5 people)
My Role
UX Researcher
UI Designer
Timeline
1 Week
TL;DR
A research-driven exploration into digital fatigue caused by Instagram Reels, focusing on why
users continue to engage in behaviours they actively want to stop.
Year
2025
2. Overview
This research examines the conflict between young adults’ well-being and the persuasive nature
of Instagram Reels, leading to significant digital fatigue. The problem is critical as many users,
despite self-realisation, succumb to platform-driven habits, leading to compulsive time-wasting
and post-scroll guilt. Multiple methods of research, such as interviews, surveys, focus groups,
shadowing, and others, were used to understand the gap between the users’ desire to change
and their inability to do so. Key findings reveal that mindless scrolling is often a compulsive
behaviour rather than a conscious choice. This is a result of a “restlessness-relief-fatigue”
feedback loop; “brain rot” content that provides valueless dopamine; its function as an escape
mechanism; and its evolution into becoming somewhat of a social obligation. This research
focuses on young adults who actively use Instagram and want to change their
mindless-scrolling routine but feel powerless to break the cycle.
=== PAGE 3 ===
3. Problem Definition
What We're Analysing
The behavioural conflict in young Instagram users who recognise their Reels scrolling is
harmful, yet cannot stop. Specifically examining:
● The "restlessness-relief-fatigue" feedback loop driving compulsive behaviour
● "Brain rot" content provides valueless dopamine hits
● Instagram as an escape mechanism and social obligation
● The gap between users' desire to change and their inability to act on it
Why It Matters
Public Health Impact: Digital fatigue and compulsive scrolling affect millions of young
adults' productivity, mental health, and well-being
Design Ethics: Reveals how persuasive platform design overrides user autonomy, even
when users are fully aware of the harm
Actionable Insights: Understanding these specific mechanisms (the feedback loop,
dopamine triggers, social pressures) provides concrete intervention points for solutions
Problem Statement: Young adults (highly active on Instagram) want to find a way to
reduce their mindless scrolling because they recognise it has become a default,
compulsive behaviour that makes them feel unproductive and guilty afterwards.
4. Methodology
We approached this as both a behavioural and design problem. We first mapped existing
research around habit loops, addictive interfaces, and digital fatigue to understand the
underlying patterns. This informed a mixed-method study: in-depth conversations (15
participants) to uncover lived experiences, surveys (35 responses) to identify recurring
behaviours, and lightweight Instagram polls (~400 responses) to capture broader trends in real
time. The goal wasn’t just to collect data, but to trace the gap between awareness and action.
=== PAGE 4 ===
5. Insights
Instagram is not used — it is fallen into. Across participants, usage wasn’t driven by need, but
by state: boredom, fatigue, or avoidance.
Scrolling is heavily concentrated at night, where self-regulation is lowest and time feels
distorted. What begins as a short break often extends into hours, especially before sleep.
Over time, Instagram has shifted from a conscious action to a reflex. Users don’t decide to open
the app — they find themselves already inside it, especially during idle moments. Content plays
a paradoxical role. Low-value, high-dopamine media (“brain-rot”) dominate consumption,
leaving users mentally drained and guilty. Yet, occasional “useful” content is used to justify
continued scrolling.
The platform is no longer purely personal — it functions as a social obligation. Users feel the
need to stay present to maintain relationships, even when they want to disengage.
Most importantly, users are not unaware. They clearly recognise their overuse, attempt to
intervene, and still relapse. Awareness exists — but it does not translate into action.
6. Conclusion
This wasn’t a problem of overuse. It was a problem of control.
Users are not unaware of their behaviour — they recognise it, resist it, and still return. This
reveals a deeper shift: interaction has moved from conscious choice to conditioned response,
shaped by design systems that operate below awareness.
What appears as “scrolling” is often something else — a response to boredom, fatigue, or
avoidance. The behaviour is automatic and difficult to articulate, because it no longer feels like a
decision.
This changes the role of design. The goal is not to limit usage, but to restore intention — to
create moments where users can recognise what they are doing, and choose differently.

Posture Corrector
=== PAGE 7 ===
Project title: Posture Corrector
Tags: Research, UX, Product Design
1. Context
Type
Group Project (5 people)
My Role
Product Designer
UX Researcher
Timeline
1 Week
TL;DR
A wearable posture-correcting device that detects slouching in real time and nudges users to sit
upright, helping build awareness and healthier sitting habits without disrupting daily routines.
Year
2024
2. Overview
This project began as an exploration into everyday physical habits — specifically, how
prolonged sitting affects posture. As part of a design module, we were asked to identify a
common, often overlooked problem and respond with a functional intervention.
Extended desk work has normalised poor posture, not because people don’t care, but because
they rarely notice it in the moment. This project focuses on making that invisible behaviour
visible, in a way that fits seamlessly into daily life.
3. Problem
Prolonged sitting has made poor posture a silent, everyday issue among desk-bound
individuals. Most people are unaware of when they begin to slouch, and existing solutions are
either intrusive, easy to ignore, or require active effort to maintain.
=== PAGE 8 ===
The challenge was to design a solution that helps people become aware of their posture in real
time, without disrupting their workflow or relying on constant conscious effort.
Problem Statement: How might we encourage better sitting posture for desk-bound workers so
that they avoid long-term back issues?
Primary Target Audience
Desk-bound workers
Office professionals
Students
Secondary Target Audience
Gamers
Elderly users
4. Insight
Posture is not ignored — it is unnoticed.
People don’t correct their posture because they don’t realise when it changes.
5. Explorations
Early concepts explored different ways of integrating posture feedback into the body —
including clip-on devices, back-mounted modules, and wearable formats.
The neckband form was chosen for its balance of comfort, stability, and proximity to the upper
spine, allowing for more accurate detection while remaining unobtrusive in daily use.
(add images)
6. Solution
A neckband-style wearable device that detects slouching in real time and alerts users to correct
their posture. Using a motion sensor to track spinal orientation, it identifies sustained deviations
from a neutral position and provides immediate feedback, helping users build awareness and
improve their sitting habits without disrupting their daily routine.

Ajanta Caves
=== PAGE 11 ===
Project title: Ajanta Caves VR simulation
Tags: Research, UX, 3D World Building, VR
1. Context
Type
Group Project (4 people)
My Role
Experience Designer
Researcher
Timeline
3 Week
TL;DR
An immersive VR experience that brings the Ajanta Caves to life, focusing on light, interaction,
and atmosphere to recreate the feeling of being inside the space.
Year
2025
2. Overview
This project is a 3D immersive simulation designed to transport users into the architectural
depth of the Ajanta Caves. Moving beyond static photography, the experience utilizes Virtual
Reality to reconstruct the scale, texture, and atmosphere of the rock-cut Buddhist monuments.
The simulation allows users to navigate the space at a 1:1 scale, offering a digital avenue for
heritage preservation and accessible exploration.
The core intent was to translate the concept of "presence" into a digital medium. This project
focuses on Atmospheric Reconstruction, using virtual reality not just to showcase the visuals,
but also tries replicate the feeling of being inside a Vihara. The design prioritises high-fidelity
textures and accurate lighting simulations to recreate the solemn, spiritual ambience of the
original site.
.
=== PAGE 12 ===
3. User Journey
Users move through a digitally reconstructed cave environment, interacting with the space in
three key ways:
● Light the Way — Use a torch to illuminate dark areas and reveal details
● Examine Artifacts — Engage with paintings and surfaces up close
● Leave Your Mark — Interact with walls by adding your own visual traces
These interactions are designed to slow the user down and encourage exploration, reinforcing
the sense of presence.

Project title: Order & Disorder
Tags: Filmmaking
1. Context
Type
Individual
My Role
Filmmaker
Sound Designer
Timeline
5 Days
TL;DR
A black-and-white video poem that explores the tension between chaos and order through a
split-screen narrative, ultimately revealing an underlying sense of structure within apparent
disorder.
Year
2025
2. Overview
Grounded in Carl Jung’s philosophy that "in all chaos there is cosmos," this video poem
explores the symbiotic tension between structure and entropy. Utilizing a split-screen diptych,
the piece forces a simultaneous confrontation between conflicting realities, highlighting the
friction between them. The decision to shoot in black and white was deliberate—stripping away
the distraction of color to focus purely on form, light, and the orchestral score. The visual
journey ultimately resolves the tension with a singular realization: that despite the apparent
noise of the cosmos, at the root of all things, there is order.

Delhi's AQI
=== PAGE 16 ===
Project title: Delhi’s AQI
Tags: Data Visualisation, Research, UI/UX
1. Context
Type
Group (2 people)
My Role
Researcher
UI/UX Designer
Timeline
1 Week
TL;DR
An interactive data visualisation that exposes how clean air in Delhi has become a privilege,
using personal data and narrative to shift users from awareness to discomfort.
Year
2026
2. Overview
Clean air in Delhi has been privatized. Upper-middle-class residents buy their way out of toxic
air through HEPA purifiers, sealed AC cars, and filtered offices — but the pollution doesn't
disappear. It stays in the shared commons, inhabited by the people who can't afford to exit it.
This is a browser-based interactive data visualization that makes this system visible. It starts by
taking inputs from users and ends with a comparative analysis.
3. Target Audience
● Age: 25–40
● Class: Upper-middle, financially independent
● Location: Delhi / NCR
● Current state: Desensitised. They've normalised the crisis through capital — purifiers,
AC commutes, sealed offices.
=== PAGE 17 ===
● Why them: They have the social capital, economic power, and political voice to demand
systemic change. They are the audience most capable of acting — and the least
confronted with the need to.
4. Data & Methodology
Weighted Daily Exposure (TWA)
E = (C1 x t1 + C2 x t2 + ... + Cn x tn) / 24
Time-weighted average across home, commute, and workplace over 24 hrs. Each environment
has a different PM2.5 concentration.
Cigarette Equivalent / Day
Cigarettes/day = Daily PM2.5 (ug/m3) / 22
Berkeley Earth equivalence formula. Translates invisible pollution into a tangible health
comparison — 22 ug/m3 sustained for one day equals one cigarette.
Life Expectancy Loss
Years Lost = ((PM2.5 - 5) / 10) x 0.98
AQLI methodology (U. of Chicago EPIC). Every 10 ug/m3 above the WHO guideline of 5 ug/m3
reduces life expectancy by ~1 year. Applied across the residency duration.

Interactive Folk Art Map
=== PAGE 19 ===
Project Title: Interactive Folk Art Map
Tags: Research, UX, UI
1. Context
Type
Individual
My Role
UI/UX Designer (intern)
Timeline
3 Months
TL;DR
An interactive kiosk interface designed to complement a physical map of Indian folk art
Year
2024
2. Challenge
The Sixth Element School installed a massive physical map of India, intricately detailed with
diverse, traditional state art. While visually striking, the physical map alone couldn't convey the
rich history, techniques, and cultural significance behind each regional style.
The challenge was to design a continuous, on-site digital interface that would sit directly in front
of the installation. It needed to act as an intuitive educational companion, allowing students and
visitors to dive deeper into the artwork they were looking at in the physical space.
3. Solution
An interactive, kiosk-style digital interface designed for an always-on display. The UI serves as
an explorative tool where users can navigate through different states and art forms, bridging the
gap between the static physical map and dynamic digital context.
Key Design Considerations:
=== PAGE 20 ===
● Glanceable & Explorative: Designed for an environment where users might engage for
30 seconds or 5 minutes. The navigation had to be instantly intuitive for various age
groups within the school.
● Visual Harmony: The UI needed to complement the vibrant traditional art (like
Madhubani) without overpowering it, using the screen to spotlight the physical piece
rather than distract from it.
● On-Site Ergonomics: Accounting for the physical placement of the screen relative to
the map to ensure a seamless "look up, look down" user experience.
Note: The final deployed version includes modifications from the original concepts to
accommodate technical feasibility.
