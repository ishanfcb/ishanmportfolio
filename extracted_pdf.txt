Digital  Fatigue  by  Instagram
 

---PAGE---

PROJECT  TITLE:   Digital  Fatigue  via  Instagram  (UX  Research)     PROJECT  DESCRIPTION:    Abstract   This  research  examines  the  conflict  between  young  adults’  well-being  and  the  persuasive  nature  
of
 
Instagram
 
Reels,
 
leading
 
to
 
significant
 
digital
 
fatigue.
 
The
 
problem
 
is
 
critical
 
as
 
many
 
users,
 
despite
 
self-realisation,
 
succumb
 
to
 
platform-driven
 
habits,
 
leading
 
to
 
compulsive
 
time-wasting
 
and
 
post-scroll
 
guilt.
 
Multiple
 
methods
 
of
 
research,
 
such
 
as
 
interviews,
 
surveys,
 
focus
 
groups,
 
shadowing,
 
and
 
others,
 
were
 
used
 
to
 
understand
 
the
 
gap
 
between
 
the
 
users’
 
desire
 
to
 
change
 
and
 
their
 
inability
 
to
 
do
 
so.
 
Key
 
findings
 
reveal
 
that
 
mindless
 
scrolling
 
is
 
often
 
a
 
compulsive
 
behaviour
 
rather
 
than
 
a
 
conscious
 
choice.
 
This
 
is
 
a
 
result
 
of
 
a
 
“restlessness-relief-fatigue”
 
feedback
 
loop;
 
“brain
 
rot”
 
content
 
that
 
provides
 
valueless
 
dopamine;
 
its
 
function
 
as
 
an
 
escape
 
mechanism;
 
and
 
its
 
evolution
 
into
 
becoming
 
somewhat
 
of
 
a
 
social
 
obligation.
 
This
 
research
 
focuses
 
on
 
young
 
adults
 
who
 
actively
 
use
 
Instagram
 
and
 
want
 
to
 
change
 
their
 
mindless-scrolling
 
routine
 
but
 
feel
 
powerless
 
to
 
break
 
the
 
cycle.
   Methodology   Primary  Research  Physical  Interviews  Sample  Size:  15  participants   Method:  Semi-structured,  in-person  interviews  were  conducted  using  a  custom  questionnaire.  
Questions
 
focused
 
on
 
Instagram
 
usage
 
habits,
 
emotional
 
responses,
 
and
 
experiences
 
of
 
digital
 
fatigue.
   Online  Surveys  Tool:  Google  Forms  Sample  Size:  35  responses   Topics:  Frequency  of  Instagram  use,  self-reported  digital  fatigue,  emotional  impacts,  and  
regulatory
 
behaviours.
 
Responses
 
were
 
anonymous
 
to
 
encourage
 
honesty.
   Instagram  Story  Polls  Platform:  Instagram  Stories  

---PAGE---

Responses:  Approximately  400  participants   Format:  The  poll  question  posted  on  Instagram  Stories  was,  “What  do  you  use  Instagram  for?”  
Poll
 
options
 
included
 
personal
 
use,
 
content
 
creation,
 
business/marketing,
 
and
 
shopping.
 
Analytics
 
for
 
response
 
counts
 
and
 
percentages
 
were
 
tracked
 
using
 
Instagram’s
 
built-in
 
insights.
  Insights  and  Interpretations    Hours  Spent  on  Instagram:  Insight:  Instagram  use  is  highly  fragmented  across  the  day,  but  disproportionately  concentrated  
at
 
night
 
due
 
to
 
lowered
 
self-regulation
 
and
 
lack
 
of
 
accountability.
  Evidence:  Participants  report  3–6+  hours/day,  with  long  sessions  especially  before  bed.  
Night-time
 
is
 
the
 
most
 
common
 
use
 
of
 
the
 
window.
   Routine  and  Habit  Formation:  Insight:  Instagram  has  evolved  from  a  voluntary  check-in  to  a  reflexive  bedtime  ritual,  triggered  
without
 
conscious
 
intention.
  Evidence:  Participants  describe  opening  IG  automatically  “out  of  habit,”  especially  during  idle  
moments
 
and
 
before
 
sleep.
 
Attempts
 
to
 
set
 
boundaries
 
(timers,
 
limits)
 
are
 
routinely
 
overridden.
   Content  Type:  Insight:  Low-information  “brain-rot”  content  produces  high  dopamine  but  yields  guilt  and  mental  
fatigue,
 
whereas
 
high-utility
 
content
 
does
 
not.
  Evidence:  Participants  explicitly  mention  certain  reels  as  “killing  your  brain”  or  “mentally  
draining,”
 
contrasting
 
them
 
with
 
“tech”
 
or
 
educational
 
content,
 
which
 
feels
 
productive.
   Purpose  of  using  Instagram:  Insight:  Instagram  has  shifted  from  personal  use  to  a  social  obligation  —  a  space  users  feel  they  
must
 
occupy.
  Evidence:  Many  open  the  app  primarily  to  check  messages  or  maintain  college/peer  
relationships.
 
Some
 
cannot
 
delete
 
IG
 
due
 
to
 
network
 
dependency.
    Self-Realisation:  Awareness  Without  Change  Insight:  Users  possess  strong  self-awareness  of  overuse  but  cannot  convert  that  awareness  into  
behaviour
 
change.
 

---PAGE---

 Evidence:  Individuals  acknowledge  the  problem,  uninstall  the  app  temporarily,  or  use  blockers  
—
 
yet
 
relapse
 
due
 
to
 
habit
 
strength
 
and
 
low-energy
 
states.
 
Timers
 
and
 
grayscale
 
modes
 
are
 
repeatedly
 
bypassed.
   Content  Value  &  Perception:  Insight:  Users  justify  continued  usage  by  pointing  to  pockets  of  “valuable  content”  (tech,  design,  
reflective
 
posts),
 
even
 
though
 
most
 
consumed
 
content
 
is
 
low-value.
  Evidence:  Participants  describe  occasional  informational  posts  as  productive,  using  them  to  
rationalise
 
prolonged
 
scrolling.
  Conclusion   Throughout  this  research,  it  became  evident  that  the  relationship  between  young  adults  and  
Instagram
 
Reels
 
is
 
far
 
more
 
complex
 
than
 
simple
 
overuse.
 
What
 
began
 
as
 
a
 
search
 
for
 
reasons
 
behind
 
digital
 
fatigue
 
evolved
 
into
 
an
 
exploration
 
of
 
habit,
 
emotion
 
and
 
design.
  What  surprised  the  team  most  was  the  level  of  self-awareness  among  users;  they  clearly  
recognised
 
their
 
compulsive
 
scrolling
 
patterns,
 
yet
 
continued
 
to
 
engage
 
with
 
the
 
platform.
 
This
 
contradiction
 
revealed
 
how
 
persuasive
 
design
 
and
 
algorithm
 
subtly
 
override
 
personal
 
intention.
  One  major  challenge  of  the  study  was  capturing  authentic  user  behaviour  beyond  self-reported  
responses.
 
Many
 
participants
 
found
 
it
 
difficult
 
to
 
articulate
 
why
 
they
 
scrolled,
 
proving
 
how
 
unconscious
 
the
 
act
 
of
 
scrolling
 
has
 
become.
 
Observational
 
methods
 
such
 
as
 
shadowing
 
helped
 
bridge
 
this
 
gap,
 
but
 
it
 
also
 
revealed
 
the
 
complexity
 
of
 
studying
 
behaviours
 
that
 
have
 
now
 
become
 
second
 
nature.
 
  The  next  phase  of  the  research  could  be  centred  around  designing  mindful  strategies  that  
emphasise
 
intentional
 
engagement
 
rather
 
than
 
passive
 
consumption.

Posture  Corrector  

---PAGE---

PROJECT  TITLE:   Posture  Corrector   PROJECT  DESCRIPTION:   A  neckband-style  wearable  device  that  aims  to  fix  posture  problems.  This  gadget  targets  people  
who
 
spend
 
a
 
lot
 
of
 
time
 
sitting.
 
It
 
uses
 
a
 
6-axis
 
IMU
 
to
 
track
 
spinal
 
orientation
 
and
 
detect
 
deviation
 
from
 
a
 
neutral
 
posture.
 
SitRight
 
makes
 
users
 
notice
 
when
 
they're
 
slouching
 
and
 
nudges
 
them
 
to
 
sit
 
up
 
straight.
 
The
 
device
 
is
 
light,
 
comfy,
 
and
 
doesn't
 
need
 
an
 
app.
 
It
 
helps
 
people
 
develop
 
better
 
sitting
 
habits
 
without
 
messing
 
up
 
their
 
daily
 
routine.
  Problem  Statement  How  might  we  encourage  better  sitting  posture  for  desk-bound  workers  so  that  they  avoid  
long-term
 
back
 
issues?
  Objective  Develop  a  wearable  device  that  alerts  users  when  they  slouch,  helping  improve  posture  and  
prevent
 
back
 
pain.
  Target  Audience:   Primary:  Desk-bound  workers  Office  workers  Students   Secondary:  Elderly  people  Gamers   Why?   Office  workers  and  desk-bound  professionals  are  most  at  risk  of  slouching  for  long  hours,  often  
without
 
realizing
 
it.
 
They're
 
the
 
ones
 
who
 
need
 
simple,
 
non-disruptive
 
support
 
the
 
most.
  Working   The  posture  indicator  uses  an  MPU6050  sensor  connected  to  an  Arduino  to  detect  upper  body  
movement
 
along
 
the
 
X-axis.
 
When
 
the
 
user
 
slouches,
 
the
 
sensor
 
registers
 
a
 
change
 
in
 
angle.
 
We’ve
 
set
 
threshold
 
values
 
and
 
a
 
time
 
delay
 
of
 
6
 
seconds
 
to
 
filter
 
out
 
temporary
 
movements
 
like
 
bending
 
to
 
pick
 
something
 
up.
 
If
 
poor
 
posture
 
is
 
maintained
 
for
 
more
 
than
 
6
 
seconds,
 
the
 
device
 
makes
 
a
 
noise,
 
prompting
 
the
 
user
 
to
 
sit
 
up
 
straight.

Del-AQI  

---PAGE---

 
Overview  
Delhi's  air  didn't  get  cleaner.  You  just  stopped  breathing  it.  
Clean  air  in  Delhi  has  been  privatised.  Upper-middle-class  residents  buy  their  way  out  of  toxic  
air
 
through
 
HEPA
 
purifiers,
 
sealed
 
AC
 
cars,
 
and
 
filtered
 
offices
 
—
 
but
 
the
 
pollution
 
doesn't
 
disappear.
 
It
 
stays
 
in
 
the
 
shared
 
commons,
 
breathed
 
by
 
the
 
people
 
who
 
can't
 
afford
 
to
 
exit
 
it.
 
The  Privilege  Shield  is  a  browser-based  interactive  data  visualisation  that  makes  this  system  
visible.
 
It
 
takes
 
the
 
viewer
 
through
 
a
 
three-phase
 
personal
 
narrative
 
—
 
from
 
what's
 
already
 
in
 
their
 
body,
 
to
 
how
 
much
 
they
 
spend
 
to
 
stay
 
protected,
 
to
 
what
 
that
 
protection
 
costs
 
someone
 
else.
 
 
The  Problem  
Existing  AQI  dashboards  fail  because  they  show  numbers  people  have  already  normalised.  A  
32-year-old
 
upper-middle-class
 
Delhiite
 
has
 
seen
 
"AQI
 
450:
 
Hazardous"
 
so
 
many
 
times
 
it
 
has
 
stopped
 
meaning
 
anything.
 
They
 
check
 
it,
 
close
 
the
 
app,
 
and
 
go
 
back
 
to
 
their
 
Dyson
 
purifier.
 
The  challenge  wasn't  awareness.  It  was  rupturing  normalisation  —  specifically  for  an  audience  
that
 
has
 
individually
 
solved
 
the
 
problem
 
for
 
themselves
 
and
 
moved
 
on.
 
Key  insight:  Standard  data  visualisations  address  the  wrong  emotional  
mechanism.
 
Showing
 
how
 
bad
 
the
 
air
 
is
 
produces
 
anxiety
 
that
 
resolves
 
into
 
a
 
purchase.
 
This
 
project
 
needed
 
to
 
produce
 
structural
 
dissonance
 
—
 
the
 
realisation
 
that
 
there
 
is
 
no
 
individual
 
fix,
 
and
 
that
 
the
 
system
 
requires
 
someone
 
else
 
to
 
be
 
unprotected.
 
 
Target  Audience  
●  Age:  25–40  ●  Class:  Upper-middle,  financially  independent  ●  Location:  Delhi  /  NCR  ●  Current  state:  Desensitised.  They've  normalised  the  crisis  through  capital  —  purifiers,  
AC
 
commutes,
 
sealed
 
offices.
 ●  Why  them:  They  have  the  social  capital,  economic  power,  and  political  voice  to  demand  
systemic
 
change.
 
They
 
are
 
the
 
audience
 
most
 
capable
 
of
 
acting
 
—
 
and
 
the
 
least
 
confronted
 
with
 
the
 
need
 
to.
 

---PAGE---

 
 
Conceptual  Framework  
Guilt  vs.  Dissonance  
The  project  was  deliberately  designed  to  produce  dissonance ,  not  guilt .  
Guilt  says:  you  are  a  bad  person  for  having  a  purifier.  The  psychological  exit  from  guilt  is  
self-absolution
 
—
 
feel
 
bad,
 
donate
 
somewhere,
 
move
 
on.
 
The
 
system
 
is
 
undisturbed.
 
Dissonance  says:  the  system  that  protects  you  requires  someone  else  to  be  unprotected.  There  
is
 
no
 
individual
 
fix.
 
The
 
only
 
resolution
 
is
 
structural
 
change.
 
Every  design  decision  —  including  the  deliberate  absence  of  a  call  to  action  at  the  end  —  was  
made
 
to
 
produce
 
dissonance
 
rather
 
than
 
guilt.
 
The  Central  Thesis  
Your  purifier  doesn't  clean  the  air.  It  lets  you  leave.  The  people  who  build  your  city  
and
 
bring
 
your
 
groceries
 
couldn't
 
come
 
with
 
you.
 
 
The  Experience  —  Three  Phases  
Phase  1  —  The  Audit  
The  viewer  inputs  their  daily  routine:  years  in  Delhi,  hours  outdoors,  home  filtration,  workspace  
filtration,
 
commute
 
mode.
 
Using  a  Time-Weighted  Average  (TWA)  calculation  across  each  environment,  the  system  
outputs:
 
●  Estimated  PM2.5  deposited  in  lung  tissue  —  across  their  years  in  Delhi,  converted  to  
milligrams
 ●  Cigarette  equivalent  per  day  —  using  the  Berkeley  Earth  formula  (Daily  PM2.5  ÷  22)  ●  Estimated  life  expectancy  loss  —  8.2  years  for  Delhi,  per  AQLI  2025  ●  Annual  spend  on  clean  air  protection  —  purifier  +  filtration  costs  amortised  ●  Disease  risk  elevation  —  heart  disease,  stroke,  COPD,  lung  cancer  (Burnett  et  al.  
2014)
 
The  results  surface  without  drama.  Just  the  number,  a  label,  and  a  source.  The  weight  comes  
from
 
precision.
 

---PAGE---

 
Phase  2  —  The  Choice  
A  single  question  on  screen:  
"Who  in  your  daily  life  do  you  interact  with  the  most?"  
Four  options  —  each  framed  as  a  relationship,  not  a  job  category:  
●  The  person  who  delivers  your  food  and  groceries  ●  The  person  who  helps  run  your  home  ●  The  person  who  guards  your  building  ●  The  person  who  sells  you  things  on  the  street  
Phase  3  —  The  Receipt  
A  two-column  receipt.  Left:  PERSONAL  AIR  EXPOSURE  RECEIPT.  Right:  EXPOSURE  BILL  
—
 
UNPAID.
 
Identical  line  items,  side  by  side:  
●  Daily  exposure  profile  (commute,  indoor,  outdoor,  weighted  average)  —  in  μ g/m³  ●  Cumulative  data  (cigarette  equivalent,  life  expectancy  loss,  monthly  income,  health  
insurance)
 ●  Disease  risk  elevations  —  different  per  profile,  because  risk  scales  with  exposure  ●  Privilege  Rank  
At  the  bottom,  a  coloured  box  per  column.  The  user's:  dark  green,  explaining  their  protected  
position.
 
The
 
worker's:
 
dark
 
red,
 
explaining
 
what
 
they'll
 
pay
 
—
 
not
 
in
 
money,
 
but
 
in
 
lung
 
capacity
 
and
 
years.
 
Final  line,  below  both  columns:  
"Clean  air  was  not  distributed.  It  was  purchased."  
No  button.  No  CTA.  No  individual  resolution.  The  receipt  ends.  The  page  ends.  
 
Design  Decisions  
Visual  Language  
●  Background:  #0D0D0D  throughout  —  not  pure  black,  slightly  warm  ●  Typography:  Space  Grotesk  for  UI,  Space  Mono  for  the  receipt  —  the  shift  in  typeface  
marks
 
the
 
shift
 
from
 
personal
 
to
 
systemic
 

---PAGE---

 
●  Colour:  Near-monochromatic.  One  accent  (#8A9E8B,  muted  grey-green)  used  for  the  
user's
 
protected
 
metrics.
 
The
 
same
 
value
 
warms
 
and
 
desaturates
 
(#9E8A7A)
 
for
 
the
 
worker's
 
data.
 
The
 
colour
 
system
 
carries
 
the
 
argument.
 ●  No  red  as  alarm.  Heaviness  communicates  danger,  not  hue.  
Interaction  Principles  
●  No  filled  buttons  anywhere.  Progression  is  triggered  by  Enter  key  or  a  plain  text  
"Continue
 
→"
 
link.
 ●  No  dropdowns.  Options  appear  as  styled  text  lists  —  each  choice  feels  like  a  selection,  
not
 
a
 
form
 
field.
 ●  Sequential  input  reveal.  One  question  at  a  time,  each  fading  in  after  the  previous  is  
answered.
 
The
 
pace
 
is
 
deliberate.
 ●  The  receipt  is  fully  static.  Zero  interactive  elements.  Intentionally  inert.  The  absence  of  
anything
 
to
 
do
 
is
 
the
 
final

The  secondary  personas  are  occupational  data  composites ,  not  human  beings.  No  names,  
no
 
faces,
 
no
 
backstories,
 
no
 
suffering
 
shown
 
as
 
spectacle.
 
Both
 
columns
 
carry
 
equal
 
visual
 
weight
 
—
 
same
 
font,
 
same

same

The

The

Data  &  Methodology  
Calculations  
Weighted  Daily  Exposure  (TWA)  
E  =  (C ₁ ×t ₁  +  C ₂ ×t ₂  +  …  +  C ×t )  ÷  24  
Time-weighted  average  across  home,  commute,  and  workplace.  Each  environment  carries  a  
different
 
PM2.5
 
concentration
 
based
 
on
 
filtration
 
type.
 
Cigarette  Equivalent  
Cigarettes/day  =  Daily  PM2.5  (μ g/m³)  ÷  22  
Berkeley  Earth  equivalence  formula.  22  μ g/m³  sustained  for  one  day  equals  one  cigarette  
smoked.
 
Life  Expectancy  Loss  

---PAGE---

 
Years  Lost  =  ((PM2.5  –  5)  ÷  10)  ×  0.98  
AQLI  methodology  (University  of  Chicago  EPIC).  Every  10  μ g/m³  above  the  WHO  guideline  of  5  
μ
g/m³
 
reduces
 
life
 
expectancy
 
by
 
approximately
 
one
 
year.
 
Disease  Risk  Elevation  
Integrated  Exposure-Response  (IER)  functions  from  Burnett  et  al.  (2014).  Non-linear  models  
mapping
 
PM2.5
 
concentration
 
to
 
relative
 
risk
 
of
 
heart
 
disease,
 
stroke,
 
COPD,
 
and
 
lung
 
cancer.
 
Values
 
differ
 
between
 
user
 
and
 
worker
 
columns
 
because
 
risk
 
scales
 
with
 
cumulative
 
exposure.
